# Character Arcs

## Kael

- **Role:** Oath-bound hunter and player viewpoint.
- **Secret:** He obeyed a retreat order and abandoned civilians his company had promised to escort.
- **Desire:** Complete contracts cleanly enough that nobody asks what duty means to him.
- **Fear:** That disobedience is vanity and obedience is cowardice.
- **Lie:** Keeping a promise means following its exact words.
- **Avoided truth:** A witness is responsible for what they permit after seeing it.
- **Player influence:** Choices shape him toward Witness, Mercy, Duty, or Ash without changing his grounded voice.
- **Best ending:** He makes an oath publicly, with named witnesses and a cost he accepts.
- **Worst ending:** He destroys evidence and calls the resulting silence peace.
- **Dialogue states:** Controlled professional, personally provoked, confessional, final oath.
- **Gameplay:** Investigation observations, report choices, final covenant role.

## Sister Anwen

- **Role:** Spiritual authority, guide, and inheritor of shrine guilt.
- **Secret:** She found the erased burial register years ago and resealed it after one uncontrolled memory killed a novice.
- **Desire:** Keep Greyfen alive long enough to confess safely.
- **Fear:** Truth released without care will punish children for inherited crimes.
- **Lie:** Delay is different from concealment.
- **Avoided truth:** She has repeatedly chosen the village over the unnamed dead.
- **Player influence:** Trust grows through discretion plus honesty, not automatic agreement.
- **Best ending:** Confesses publicly and helps build a witnessed covenant.
- **Worst ending:** Becomes the next silent keeper or dies defending the seal.
- **Dialogue states:** Guarded shrine greeting, evidence recognition, cemetery admission, open confession, finale.
- **Gameplay:** Relocates to cemetery/castle/glade; grants or removes shrine access.

## Mira Fen

- **Role:** Herbalist, material skeptic, and practical beneficiary of cursed soil.
- **Secret:** She knew grave ash strengthened medicine and continued controlled cultivation.
- **Desire:** Save lives with knowledge others are too frightened to use.
- **Fear:** Moral purity will destroy useful medicine and expose her as exploitative.
- **Lie:** A cure's origin matters less than whether it works.
- **Avoided truth:** Consent matters even when the dead cannot give it.
- **Player influence:** Encourage disclosure, regulated use, or destruction.
- **Best ending:** Establishes a named memorial garden with family consent.
- **Worst ending:** Hides research until it creates another Bog Wretch.
- **Dialogue states:** Clinical deflection, defensive admission, negotiated responsibility, witness.
- **Gameplay:** Crafting support, clue analysis, soil-state changes.

## Rook

- **Role:** Smuggler, rumor broker, and descendant of a survivor.
- **Secret:** His jokes and maps conceal a family list of the dead.
- **Desire:** Learn the truth without becoming its public target.
- **Fear:** Being used as proof rather than treated as a person.
- **Lie:** Information is safe if nobody owns all of it.
- **Avoided truth:** Fragmenting testimony also protects the lie.
- **Player influence:** Earn consent, exploit ancestry, or protect anonymity.
- **Best ending:** Testifies on his own terms and redraws the road openly.
- **Worst ending:** Flees, is exposed without consent, or sells the final map.
- **Dialogue states:** Frightened humor, clipped seriousness, anger, voluntary testimony.
- **Gameplay:** Alternate routes, map fragments, bandit approach.

## Lord Edric Vargan

- **Role:** Inheritor of political guilt and guardian of present stability.
- **Secret:** He found the command ledger six years ago and suppressed it after calculating that exposure would collapse Greyfen's trade and defenses.
- **Desire:** Preserve people who depend on a house built by criminals.
- **Fear:** Public truth will satisfy the dead by destroying the living.
- **Lie:** Stewardship gives him the right to decide what others may know.
- **Avoided truth:** Concealment made the inherited crime his own choice.
- **Player influence:** Cooperation, public exposure, or compelled testimony.
- **Best ending:** Testifies voluntarily, surrenders title, and organizes restitution.
- **Worst ending:** Dies defending the ledger or survives as architect of a cleaner lie.
- **Dialogue states:** Courteous control, precise denial, private admission, stripped-down fear, testimony.
- **Gameplay:** Castle access, guards, supplies, final support.

## Widow Elna

- **Role:** Personal face of loving someone guilty.
- **Secret:** She knew Harl helped bar the gate but invented a heroic death for their children.
- **Desire:** Preserve one good memory without denying his victims.
- **Fear:** Truth will erase the man she loved rather than judge what he did.
- **Lie:** A kinder story harms nobody.
- **Avoided truth:** The bell rings because Harl wanted his name attached to his act.
- **Player influence:** Truth, mercy, or direct memory encounter.
- **Best ending:** Names both his care and his cowardice.
- **Worst ending:** Burns his token and repeats the heroic lie.
- **Gameplay:** Bell quest, grave state, testimony.

## Blacksmith Tor

- **Role:** Craftsman whose useful inheritance contains stolen iron.
- **Secret:** He recognized old refugee marks on his stock and ground them away.
- **Desire:** Keep tools in working hands.
- **Fear:** Restitution will ruin his forge and punish apprentices.
- **Lie:** Metal has no memory.
- **Avoided truth:** He erased evidence for convenience.
- **Player influence:** Memorial nails, continued use with disclosure, or surrender.
- **Best ending:** Turns inherited iron into named memorial work while maintaining the forge.
- **Worst ending:** Melts evidence into weapons for Edric.
- **Gameplay:** Crafting prices, forge visuals, equipment rewards.

## Farmer Toma

- **Role:** Ordinary villager showing present-day compromise.
- **Secret:** He feeds a Stalker that saved his daughter from bandits.
- **Desire:** Protect his family without inviting village judgment.
- **Fear:** Any admitted contact with Wychwood will make his child an outcast.
- **Lie:** Private gratitude creates no public danger.
- **Avoided truth:** His secrecy has cost neighboring farmers livestock.
- **Player influence:** Relocation, killing, or regulated care.
- **Best ending:** Confesses and helps create a guarded forest boundary.
- **Worst ending:** The creature is killed after his lies provoke panic.
- **Gameplay:** Alternate encounter, trap reward, farm-state changes.

## The White Hart

- **Role:** Supernatural witness and dangerous memory without human proportion.
- **Secret:** It agreed to hide the road because shrine keepers promised no blood would be shed there.
- **Desire:** End its binding and force every beneficiary to remember.
- **Fear:** Not death, but another covenant that turns witness into silence.
- **Lie:** Complete memory is the same as justice.
- **Avoided truth:** It cannot understand forgiveness without forgetting.
- **Player influence:** Demonstrate witnessed human accountability, offer a new keeper, release, or destroy it.
- **Best ending:** Released after living witnesses assume judgment.
- **Worst ending:** Destroyed or rebound through another hidden oath.
- **Dialogue states:** Distant omen, memory fragments, direct challenge, negotiated covenant.
- **Gameplay:** Environmental reactions, optional boss, ending state.

## Ghoulkin As Thematic Force

- **Role:** Physical symptoms of denied identity.
- **Origin:** Animal and human remains animated by fragments attracted to erased names and personal tokens.
- **Desire:** Not conscious revenge; compulsive completion of broken memory.
- **Fear:** Stabilization through true naming disperses them.
- **Lie embodied:** The dead can be made anonymous without consequence.
- **Player influence:** Kill vessels, recover evidence, or use names to interrupt them.
- **Best resolution:** Fewer form as names are restored.
- **Worst resolution:** Destruction scatters memory into soil and creates new variants.
- **Gameplay:** Clue-dependent staging, aftermath evidence, named weakness interaction.
