# Revised character arcs

| Character | Ordinary life and want | Present contradiction | Playable turn and continuing cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kael | Helps lift a cart, asks plain questions, wants a bounded job | Calls obedience a sufficient account of leaving civilians | Freely stays with living witnesses; confesses a separate retreat; shapes protection, record, and final burden without a hidden destiny |
| Anwen | Gives a traveler a cup and keeps Oren's coat | Protects children through concealment that keeps names disordered | Admits failed containment and novice's death; separates food from rites; offers or refuses her own testimony |
| Rook | Helps the cart with a joke, remembers Bram's generosity | Wants a usable road and ownership of family testimony | Protected copies permit voluntary speech; public family marks expose him without consent; destroying the map also deprives future travelers |
| Edric | Releases grain for sick households and maintains patrols | Treats stewardship as the right to determine others' knowledge | Faces ledger and ration coercion; cooperation surrenders shelter of title, exposure removes authority, compulsion remains evidence rather than consent |
| Mira | Treats Toma's daughter's fever with practiced care | Useful ash treatment denied families an informed choice | Offers slower clean-root care to those who refuse; closure and disclosure have practical costs; testimony must be freely hers |
| Tor | Gives scrap and keeps doors, carts, and tools working | Ground off refugee marks for convenience | Replaces gradually with memorial work, discloses working stock, or surrenders disputed iron while keeping ordinary scrap available |
| Elna | Remembers Harl warming shoes, including an embarrassing burned one | A kinder war account concealed the people outside his gate | Names love and wrongdoing together; owns her grief and chooses public or private participation |
| Toma | Stays awake with a feverish daughter and tends the fold | Hid a dangerous fragment that once protected his child | Relocates a protection path, establishes disclosed supervision, or ends it while recording what it saved |
| Senn | Once accepted an order as explanation; now demands redress | Uses old injury to excuse present raids | Custody, exile, or execution preserve factual accountability; freely chosen testimony never buys absolution |
| Halvern | A bounded sanctuary, refusal, and execution memory | Repeats the moment authority asked him to close the road | Copied proof survives preservation, settling, or destruction; he has no knowledge of later Edric and no living consent to lend |
| White Hart | Bears the complete account and cannot lie | Confuses complete recollection with proportional justice | Accepts human responsibility without demanding blanket assent; four explicit methods change captivity, memory, and the burden on living people |

Len, the returned soldier, is a gate-and-lantern fragment. Naming or privately settling him preserves the account, not a resurrected person who can join a ritual. Ghoulkin likewise seek incomplete testimony rather than acting as articulate revenge agents.

Main-path decisions provide guaranteed facts. Optional relationships add freely offered support and unique aftermath; refusal never becomes a disguised missing-ending key. Ordinary care, an objection, an unprompted act, and a later return should carry every principal living character's development.
