# Manuscript scene ledger

The 114 rows follow the scene breaks in the supplied Markdown. Line numbers refer to that immutable copy. An owner identifies where an event, decision or consequence is represented. A row may share a playable encounter with its neighbors. The implementation ledger records which sequences have rendered input evidence.

## Adaptation choices

- The Greyfen shrine supplies Sella’s viewpoint. Kael must discover the evidence himself. Private scenes involving Rook are discovered through his admission and the crew’s handling of the schedule.
- The mill’s clean remainder depends on the player’s handling of the channels. A shut mill cannot generate grain. The novel’s single plan for the road remains one possible route through the established physical crossings.
- The new quest edition gives Tor’s injury and the culvert lasting work consequences. Rook can object to Kael and remain in his home. Conscription, forgiveness and public disclosure are separate choices.
- The original garden quest featured a single prisoner. The new edition puts the three seasonal workers, the surrendered tools and Toma’s petition in its place. Previous editions keep their saved quest.
- The clerk’s duplicate can be missed. The thirty-two-year order, Edric’s later suppression and the eight-year escort have distinct evidence. The Hart can speak only for the road it witnessed.
- The Assembly, worker custody and voluntary keeper services keep the existing alternate resolutions. The second ferry rescue, calf injury and lost goods belong to Open Road. Other endings have their own spring work. Living people can still decline intimacy and an invitation can end in a meal.
- Several changes of room, interim travel days, private reflections, ward conversations, and the court journey after the hearing share activities with adjacent scenes. The original manuscript preserves their full dialogue. The six-month visit advances only by the player’s choice.
